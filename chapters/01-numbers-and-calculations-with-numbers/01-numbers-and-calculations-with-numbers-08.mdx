For each of the following questions, calculate the answer and then round it up or down depending on the situation. Explain why you rounded the way you did.

**Problem:**
Liam is packing mushrooms. There are \(\text{15}\) mushrooms in a punnet. He has \(\text{275}\) mushrooms to pack. How many punnets does he need?

![](../../assets/images/gd-0058.jpg)

**Solution:**
\(\text{275} \div \text{15} = \text{18,33}\ldots\) He can only sell full punnets, so he must round down to \(\text{18}\).

**Problem:**
A car mechanic charges \(\text{R}\,\text{200}\) per hour or part thereof for labour. How much would he charge for a job that takes \(\text{270}\) minutes?

**Solution:**
\(\text{270}\) minutes is \(\text{4}\) hours and \(\text{30}\) minutes, but he rounds up to \(\text{5}\) hours, as he charges “per hour or part thereof”. So he charges \(\text{R}\,\text{1 000}\).

**Problem:**
An amusement park ride has places for \(\text{30}\) people. \(\text{82}\) people are in the queue for the ride. How many times will the ride need to run?

**Solution:**
\(\text{82} \div \text{30} = \text{2}\) remainder \(\text{22}\). They cannot turn away customers, so they should round up and run the ride \(\text{3}\) times.

**Problem:**
Nokuthula is putting up washing lines in her yard. The distance from one pole to the other is \(\text{3,2}\) \(\text{m}\). How many lines can she put up if she has \(\text{18}\) \(\text{m}\) of washing line?

![](../../assets/images/gd-0059.jpg)

**Solution:**
\(\text{18} \div \text{3,2} = \text{5,625}\). She should round down, as she can't put up a fraction of a line, and may need more line for knots and so on. So she can hang \(\text{5}\) lengths of line.

For each of the following problems, first write two ratios equal to each other, then find the unknown value:

**Problem:**
A recipe calls for \(\frac{\text{1}}{\text{3}}\) cup sugar to \(\text{2}\) cups of flour. How many cups of flour do you need to add to \(\text{3}\) cups of sugar?

**Solution:**
\(\frac{\text{1}}{\text{3}}  : \text{2} = \text{1} : \text{6} = \text{3} : \text{18}\). So you need to add \(\text{18}\) cups of flour to \(\text{3}\) cups of sugar.

**Problem:**
A survey shows that \(\text{5}\) : \(\text{1}\) learners in a school have their own cell phone. If there are \(\text{1 350}\) learners in the school, how many do not have their own cell phone?

**Solution:**
There are \(\text{6}\) parts in the ratio, so \(\text{1}\) part \(= \text{1 350} \div \text{6} = \text{225}\) learners do not have their own cell phone.

Three litres of milk cost \(\text{R}\,\text{29,95}\) at Shop A and two litres of milk cost \(\text{R}\,\text{15,95}\) at Shop B.

**Problem:**
What is the cost per litre at each shop?

**Solution:**
Shop: A \(\text{R}\,\text{29,95} \div \text{3} = \text{R}\,\text{9,98}\). Shop B: \(\text{R}\,\text{15,95} \div \text{2} = \text{R}\,\text{7,98}\)

**Problem:**
Which is the better buy?

**Solution:**
Shop B.

**Problem:**
How much would five litres of milk cost at each shop?

![](../../assets/images/gd-0060.jpg)

**Solution:**
Shop A: \(\text{R}\,\text{9,98} \times \text{5} = \text{R}\,\text{49,90}\). Shop B: \(\text{R}\,\text{7,98} \times \text{5} = \text{R}\,\text{39,90}\)

**Problem:**
Two different sized jars of jam are sold at the following prices: A: \(\text{500}\) \(\text{g}\) for \(\text{R}\,\text{8,50}\) or B: \(\text{750}\) \(\text{g}\) for \(\text{R}\,\text{11,50}\). Which size is the better buy?

**Solution:**
Work out what \(\text{100}\) \(\text{g}\) costs for each. A: \(\text{R}\,\text{8,50} \div \text{5} = \text{R}\,\text{1,70}\) and B: \(\text{R}\,\text{11,50} \div \text{7,5} = \text{R}\,\text{1,533} \ldots\) So jar B is cheaper.
